## 2. Emergency Services

### 2.1 Overview
The Emergency Department (ED) at ECMC is open 24/7, 365 days a year. It is a Level II Trauma Center staffed by board-certified emergency physicians, trauma surgeons, nurses, and paramedics. Ambulance and helicopter landing access is available.

- **Location:** North Wing, Ground Floor, Entrance C
- **Emergency phone:** (555) 010-1911
- **Ambulance bay:** Entrance C, Lane 2
- **Helipad:** Rooftop, East Tower
- **Average wait time (non-critical):** 25–45 minutes
- **Average door-to-doctor time (critical):** under 5 minutes

### 2.2 When to Go to the Emergency Room
Go to the ED or call 911 for:
- Chest pain, pressure, or tightness
- Difficulty breathing or shortness of breath
- Signs of stroke (face drooping, arm weakness, slurred speech)
- Severe bleeding or deep wounds
- Head injury or loss of consciousness
- Severe burns
- Broken bones or major trauma
- Seizures
- Severe allergic reactions (anaphylaxis)
- Poisoning or overdose
- Sudden severe abdominal pain
- Thoughts of self-harm (call or text 988 as well)

### 2.3 Triage Levels (Emergency Severity Index)

| Level | Description | Example |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Immediate life-saving intervention | Cardiac arrest |
| 2 | High risk, should not wait | Chest pain, stroke symptoms |
| 3 | Urgent, multiple resources needed | Abdominal pain, fracture |
| 4 | Less urgent, one resource | Simple laceration |
| 5 | Non-urgent | Minor rash, sore throat |

### 2.4 Emergency Services Offered
- Trauma resuscitation and surgery
- Cardiac emergencies (STEMI/heart attack protocol, door-to-balloon target under 60 minutes)
- Stroke team (CT and thrombolysis available 24/7)
- Pediatric emergency care
- Obstetric and gynecological emergencies
- Psychiatric emergency evaluation
- Toxicology and poison management
- Burn stabilization
- Orthopedic emergency care and splinting
- On-site CT, MRI, X-ray, ultrasound, and lab

### 2.5 Fast Track and Urgent Care
- **Fast Track Clinic:** Located inside the ED for minor injuries and illnesses. Open 10:00 AM – 10:00 PM daily.
- **Urgent Care Center:** Adjacent to the main building, walk-in basis, Mon–Sun 8:00 AM – 10:00 PM. Handles colds, flu, minor cuts, sprains, UTIs, and mild infections.
- **Emergency visit cost estimate (self-pay):** $350 – $2,500 depending on severity (before tests).

### 2.6 Emergency Policy Notes
- Under federal EMTALA law, ECMC treats every patient regardless of ability to pay or insurance status.
- One family member may accompany a patient in the treatment area at the discretion of staff.
- Bring a photo ID, insurance card, and list of current medications if possible.

## 10. Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: Is the emergency room open on holidays?**
A: Yes. The Emergency Department is open 24 hours a day, 7 days a week, every day of the year, including holidays.

**Q: Do I need a referral to see a cardiologist?**
A: Not for self-pay patients. Some insurance plans (such as HMOs) require a referral, so please check with your insurer.

**Q: Which department treats back pain?**
A: Back pain can be treated by the Spine & Chiropractic Center for conservative care and by Orthopedics if surgery may be needed.

**Q: Can I get a skin cancer check without an appointment?**
A: No. Skin cancer screenings require an appointment. Call (555) 010-1400 or book via the patient portal.

**Q: Does insurance cover chiropractic care?**
A: Many plans cover a limited number of visits per year. Medicare covers spinal manipulation for certain conditions. Contact your insurer to verify.

**Q: What should I bring to the emergency room?**
A: Photo ID, insurance card, a list of medications and allergies, and your emergency contact information. If you do not have these, you will still be treated.

**Q: Does the hospital offer telehealth?**
A: Yes, for follow-up visits in cardiology, dermatology, endocrinology, and psychiatry.

**Q: Where do I park for the emergency department?**
A: Use Entrance C. Parking is free for the first 2 hours.

**Q: How do I request my medical records?**
A: Use the patient portal or call (555) 010-1060. A signed authorization and photo ID are required.

**Q: Are cosmetic dermatology procedures covered by insurance?**
A: Generally no. Medically necessary treatments (acne, eczema, psoriasis, skin cancer) are typically covered.

**Q: What is the difference between Urgent Care and the Emergency Room?**
A: Urgent Care handles non-life-threatening problems like colds, minor cuts, and sprains. The ER is for life-threatening or severe conditions such as chest pain, stroke, or major injuries.

**Q: Is there financial help for patients who cannot afford care?**
A: Yes. The charity care program offers 25%–100% discounts based on income. Visit Financial Counseling in Main Building, Room 105.
